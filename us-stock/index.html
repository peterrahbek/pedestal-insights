Why outflow is split in two

read_orders active Shopify orders are subtracted from total outflow; what remains is wholesale.

A wholesale pull of 20 units looks exactly like 20 days of retail demand in the stock figures. So every drop is classified: steady drops of 1–2 units are drip and set the cover, while large or coordinated drops are bulk and are reported separately as risk. A style with no drip can still be critical. If a wholesaler just took 20 and 20 remain, the shelf carries exactly one more pull.

Method and assumptions

Source
nmdpedestal.myshopify.com, Admin API 2025-10. One location ("Shop location"), which is Normode's warehouse in Marietta, GA.
Measurement
Daily snapshots of available stock per variant. A drop between two snapshots is outflow regardless of channel, so wholesale pulls that bypass Shopify are counted. Deltas are divided by elapsed time, so a missed snapshot does not inflate the rate.
Bulk threshold
A drop counts as bulk at 5 units or more, or at half the position when at least 4 were held. If 6 styles or more drop on the same day totalling at least 15 units, the whole day is treated as one coordinated pull.
Lead time
Assumption pending confirmation: 56 days from order to goods on the shelf in Marietta, plus 21 days of buffer. That is a conservative sea-freight guess, not a confirmed lead time. Update it in analyze.py once Normode has given the actual figure.
Inbound
No open inventory transfers into Marietta, so nothing is currently on its way. Transfers are read straight from Shopify, so this only reflects replenishment registered there.
Colours
Columns follow the canonical 2026 line. 39 units sit in legacy colours, which are to be run down rather than replenished.
Value
Stated at retail USD. US cost prices are inconsistent and are deliberately not used.
Not measured
The store is still password protected, and there are no committed or incoming units. Cadence therefore covers wholesale and manual adjustments until launch.
